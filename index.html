      How it works

      1 · Real seafloor

      16 Terrarium elevation tiles (ETOPO1 and other sources) are decoded in the browser, h = R·256 + G + B/256 − 32768, and averaged to a 512 × 512 grid of Mercator cells about 20 km across. Each row's cell size shrinks with latitude: s = 19.6 km · cos(lat).

      2 · Earthquake → initial wave

      Fault size from magnitude (Strasser et al. 2010): log₁₀L = −2.477 + 0.585·Mw, log₁₀W = −0.882 + 0.351·Mw; slip D = M₀/(μLW) with M₀ = 10^(1.5·Mw + 9.1). The seafloor (and sea surface) is lifted on the trench side and dropped on the land side:

      η₀ = 0.3·D · taper(u, L) · [ G(v − W/4) − 0.6·G(v + W/4) ]

      The 2004 preset uses the 5 published fault segments of Grilli et al. (2007), each breaking at its own time.

      3 · Shallow-water "pipes"

      Each ocean cell is a water column joined to its 4 neighbours by pipes. A height difference accelerates the pipe flow, and the flow moves water between columns:

      dQ/dt = g·h·Δη    dη/dt = (ΣQin − ΣQout) / s²

      Together they give the wave equation with speed c = √(g·h): about 200 m/s (720 km/h) over 4 km of water, slowing on shelves, which bends wave fronts. A positivity limiter, an adaptive time step (dt = 0.4·s/√(g·hmax), about 20 s), a 20-cell absorbing sponge at the edges and shallow-water friction keep it stable. It all runs in a Web Worker.

      4 · Confirmed warning time

      lead = tcity − (tfirst sensor + Tproc)

      A city counts as warned when lead ≥ Tevac. The optimizer adds sensors one at a time, each time picking the site that warns the most people summed over 15 hypothetical quakes.

      Limitations

      • ~20 km grid: harbours, bays and narrow shelves are not resolved, so arrival times at the coast are approximate (see the 2004 check).
      • Simplified dipole source, not the full Okada (1985) solution; uniform slip per segment.
      • No inundation or run-up: heights are offshore, not on land.
      • No Coriolis force, no wave dispersion, no tides.
      • Populations are approximate and mix years and area definitions.
