How many minutes does a coastal city get to run? LeadTime simulates the tsunami and shows where warning sensors buy the most.

A tsunami simulator running on the real Indian Ocean floor, live in a browser, turned into a decision tool. Place deep-ocean pressure sensors and every coastal city's confirmed warning time updates instantly. An optimizer finds where sensors protect the most people across 15 possible earthquakes. Free, open source, no login.

How it works, and how we checked it
TypeScript · Leaflet · Web Workers · no backend, no AI API

1Source

Fault length and width from magnitude (Strasser et al. 2010), slip from the seismic moment:

M0 = 101.5Mw + 9.1 = μ L W D

The seafloor and sea surface rise on the trench side and drop on the land side. The 2004 preset uses Grilli et al. 2007's 5 segments, each breaking at its published time.

2Solver

Each ocean cell is a water column joined to its neighbours by pipes:

dQ/dt = ghΔη,  dη/dt = ΣQ/s²  ⇒  c = √gh

About 200 m/s over 4 km of water, slower over shelves, so fronts bend into beams. One signed flux per face, a positivity limiter, CFL time step, absorbing sponge, shallow-water friction.

3Warning

A city's confirmed warning time is when the wave arrives, minus when the first sensor detects it plus processing:

lead = tcity − (tfirst sensor + Tproc)

Warned if lead ≥ Tevac (defaults 10 and 15 min, sliders). The optimizer adds the site that warns the most people summed over all 15 scenarios.

4Does it match 2004?

Observed: Merrifield 2005; Nagarajan 2006; Tsuji 2006; Bilham 2005. Simulated: Science tab, nearest ocean cell, 5 cm threshold. Hollow = soft checks, not in the error.
Tide gaugeObservedSimulatedΔ min
Mean absolute error 20 min over 13 gauges; 12 of 13 early. A 20 km grid cannot resolve harbours and shallow shelves, where waves slow most. The warning tool reads cities further offshore (error 26 min, always early), so warning times err on the short, safe side. We report this rather than tune it away.

8Limits, stated plainly

  • 20 km grid: coastal arrival times are approximate (see 4).
  • Simplified source: a dipole, not the full Okada (1985) solution.
  • No inundation, Coriolis, dispersion or tides; heights are offshore.
  • Hypothetical scenarios for the optimizer; not an operational forecast.
